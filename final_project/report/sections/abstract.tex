\begin{abstract}
Quantization is the standard way to fit large language models into GPU memory, but a smaller
model is not automatically a faster one. We measure the memory--performance trade-off end to end
on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 for Qwen2.5 models with 3B, 7B and 14B parameters in eight
configurations: BF16, LLM.int8() and NF4 on Hugging Face Transformers, and BF16, FP8 W8A8,
GPTQ-INT8, AWQ-INT4 and GPTQ-INT4 on vLLM. The study covers \NumMeasurements{} timed runs, a
replay of 300 ShareGPT requests, WikiText-2 perplexity and GSM8K accuracy. Real weight savings fall
short of the nominal ratio (\WtNFSeven{} rather than $4\times$ for 4-bit weights on the 7B model)
and shrink further at run time because the KV cache and activations stay in 16 bits. Speed depends
on the kernel and the regime rather than on the bit-width: at batch size one the same 4-bit weights
decode \AWQspeedup{} faster than BF16 with vLLM's Marlin kernels, while bitsandbytes LLM.int8() is
\INTeightslowdown{} slower. Weight-only formats stop paying off once batching makes decoding
compute-bound, whereas FP8 W8A8 stays faster everywhere and delivers \ThrFPratio{} the BF16
throughput at batch size 256. Kernel microbenchmarks, a measured roofline and per-step profiles
explain every crossover. \QualityAbstract{} We release the harness, the raw measurements and a
decision guide for practitioners.
\end{abstract}
