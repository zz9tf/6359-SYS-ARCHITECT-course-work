Replaying 300 ShareGPT requests (\cref{fig:serving,tab:serving}; median prompt 31 tokens, median
answer 284 tokens) confirms the controlled sweep under a realistic, decode-heavy mix. With one
client the median TPOT falls from \SrvTPOTbfOne\,ms with BF16 to \SrvTPOTawqOne\,ms with AWQ-INT4
and \SrvTPOTfpOne\,ms with FP8. As clients are added, continuous batching~\cite{yu2022orca} raises
the throughput of every format, and the ranking shifts toward FP8: at 64 clients FP8 serves
\SrvThrFP{} output tokens/s against \SrvThrAWQ{} for AWQ-INT4 and \SrvThrBF{} for BF16. The serving
stack matters even more than the number format: with the same 16 requests in flight, vLLM BF16
delivers \SrvGap{} the throughput of HF BF16 with static batching, and HF's quantized
configurations are slower still (\SrvHFnf{} tokens/s for NF4 and \SrvHFint{} for LLM.int8()).
Static batching pads every batch to its longest prompt and holds it until its longest answer is
done, so its median TTFT is \SrvTTFThf\,ms against \SrvTTFTvlSixteen\,ms with continuous
batching.
