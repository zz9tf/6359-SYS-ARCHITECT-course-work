\answer{Yes. The serving stack matters even more than the format.}
A replay of 300 ShareGPT requests repeats the batch-size story (\cref{tab:serving_main}). With one client, decoding is memory-bound, so AWQ-INT4 gives the lowest TPOT
(\SrvTPOTawqOne\,ms vs.\ \SrvTPOTbfOne\,ms for BF16). With 64 clients it becomes compute-bound, so FP8
gives the highest throughput (\SrvThrFP{} vs.\ \SrvThrBF{} tokens/s). The stack matters more: with
the same 16 requests in flight and the same BF16 weights, vLLM delivers \SrvGap{} the throughput of
HF, whose static batches wait for their longest request. This gap comes from continuous
batching~\cite{yu2022orca} and CUDA graphs, not from quantization (the stacks are timed differently;
\cref{app:real}).
