\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}
\emph{One GPU, two model families.} All results come from a single RTX 5090 and dense Qwen
models (Qwen2.5 in three sizes, Qwen3-8B as a check). The mechanism we identify is architectural
(bytes per weight against arithmetic intensity), and Qwen3-8B reproduces it, so the qualitative
picture should carry over to other dense models and GPUs, but the crossover batch sizes scale with
each GPU's FLOP-to-bandwidth ratio and must be re-measured. Mixture-of-experts models, whose decode
reads only a fraction of the weights, are outside our scope.
\emph{Display GPU.} The GPU also drives a desktop; the driver's watchdog aborts any kernel that runs
for several seconds, which cost us one cell (LLM.int8() prefill of 16 prompts of 2048 tokens on the
14B model).
\emph{Library versions.} Kernel coverage moves fast: newer bitsandbytes releases may close part of the
gap we measure, and vLLM picks kernels per GPU architecture. We pin every version and release the
harness so the study can be re-run.
\emph{Uncontrolled clocks.} We could not lock clocks without administrator rights. Repetitions agree closely (\cref{sec:method}), and we report means.
\emph{Synthetic prompts.} Random token ids exercise exactly the same kernels as natural text in a
dense model, but they are out of distribution, which is why quality is measured separately on
WikiText-2 and GSM8K and speed on real ShareGPT traffic.
\emph{Greedy decoding.} Quality numbers use greedy decoding, the single most common evaluation
setting; sampling would add variance but would not change the cost of a step.
