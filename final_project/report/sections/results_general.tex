\answer{The systems effects carry over to a newer model family. The quality order of the formats does
not, so it must be measured for each model.}

\textbf{Qwen3-8B.} If the speed effects come from the hardware and the kernels, they should not depend
on the model. Qwen3-8B tests this with a different shape: \FamRLayers{} layers and \FamRKVheads{} KV
heads, against \FamQLayers{} and \FamQKVheads{} for Qwen2.5-7B. The systems effects carry over
(\cref{fig:general}a, \cref{app:general}): at batch size~1, AWQ-INT4 decodes \FamRAWQone{} faster than
BF16 (Qwen2.5-7B: \FamQAWQone), FP8 \FamRFPone, and LLM.int8() runs at \FamRINTone{} of the BF16 speed.
The one change confirms the host-overhead explanation: with more layers, NF4 falls from \FamQNFone{} to
\FamRNFone{} of the BF16 speed. Quality, in contrast, depends on the weights rather than on the
kernels. \FamQualitySentence

\textbf{Qwen3-32B.} Spending memory on parameters matters most when a model would not fit
otherwise. Qwen3-32B needs \BigBFw\,GB in BF16 and about half of that in 8 bits, more than the GPU's
32\,GB, so it runs here only in 4 bits (AWQ-INT4: \BigAWQw\,GB plus
\BigAWQkv{} cached tokens). Compared with Qwen3-8B in BF16 (\SmallBFw\,GB), it costs \BigAWQtpot{} vs.\
\SmallBFtpot\,ms per token, but it scores \BigGSMawq\% vs.\ \SmallGSMbf\% on GSM8K (\BigGAWQp) and
\BigMMLUawq\% vs.\ \SmallMMLUbf\% on MMLU (\BigMAWQp; \cref{fig:general}c). \BigVerdict{}
