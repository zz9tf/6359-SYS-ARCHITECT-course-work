\answer{The systems results carry over to a newer model family. The quality order of the formats
does not, so it must be measured for each model.}

\textbf{Qwen3-8B} has \FamRLayers{} layers and \FamRKVheads{} KV heads (Qwen2.5-7B: \FamQLayers{} and
\FamQKVheads) and runs in non-thinking mode. The systems results carry over (\cref{fig:general}a,
\cref{app:general}): at batch size~1, AWQ-INT4 decodes \FamRAWQone{} faster than BF16 (Qwen2.5-7B:
\FamQAWQone), FP8 \FamRFPone{}, and LLM.int8() runs at \FamRINTone{} of the BF16 speed. The one
difference supports the host-overhead explanation: NF4, \FamQNFone{} faster on the 28-layer
Qwen2.5-7B, runs at \FamRNFone{} of the BF16 speed on the 36-layer Qwen3-8B. \FamQualitySentence

\textbf{Qwen3-32B} raises a different question: does the model fit at all? Its BF16 weights need
\BigBFw\,GB and 8-bit weights about half of that, more than the GPU's 32\,GB, so it runs only in 4
bits (AWQ-INT4: \BigAWQw\,GB plus \BigAWQkv{} cached tokens; NF4: \BigNFw\,GB). Compared with
Qwen3-8B in BF16 (\SmallBFw\,GB; \cref{fig:general}c), it needs \BigAWQtpot{} vs.\ \SmallBFtpot\,ms per
token at batch size~1 and scores \BigGSMawq\% vs.\ \SmallGSMbf\% on GSM8K (\BigGAWQp) and
\BigMMLUawq\% vs.\ \SmallMMLUbf\% on MMLU (\BigMAWQp). \BigVerdict{} (With the checkpoint's FP16
activations, \BigNaNwin{} perplexity windows overflow; BF16 activations fix this.)
